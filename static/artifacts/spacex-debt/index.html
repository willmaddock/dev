Tap a bar for what happened and the source, then try the debt-ceiling calculator. Figures are approximate.

Solid bar: reported by the source. Dashed bar: derived by me from reported totals, shown as a floor.

These are the four main swings, not a full ledger: there is no bar for the climb between the August rally and Oct 6, so the bars do not add up to a net change. Measured directly, SpaceX’s value of under $2T on Oct 30 (NewsBytes) is still more than $600B below its $2.64T close on June 16 (Bloomberg). Outlets differ on market value because they use different share counts, so I show swings rather than a single peak figure. The late-June-to-July slide is derived: sources report a total decline of more than $1T from the peak by mid-to-late July, so I subtracted the June crash and show the result as a floor.

Why this is not a forecast. Borrowing is uneven (tax dates, interest payments), and the limit applies to debt subject to the limit, not exactly total debt. Analysts at the Bipartisan Policy Center expect the limit to be reached between late winter and mid-summer 2027. Extraordinary measures can delay that date, so a real deadline would fall later. The $8.3B figure is my own arithmetic from two rounded numbers ($40.0T on Aug 19 to $40.59T on Oct 29), so treat it as approximate.
Reported: the $41.1T limit, total debt of $40.59T on Oct 29 (Treasury) and $40T on Aug 19 (AP). My own estimates: the straight-line date, the Aug–Oct pace (computed from those two debt figures), and the slower and faster paces, which are illustrative.
